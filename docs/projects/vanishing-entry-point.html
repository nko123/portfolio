The Vanishing Entry Point

The data behind a job market that got harder for new graduates.

Data viz
D3
Labor economics
Why the job market got harder for new grads. Five public datasets in an interactive story with Chart.js and a D3 explorer of 74 majors.

May 1, 2026

The question

More people hold bachelor’s degrees than ever, yet new graduates say the first job is harder to land. We set out to check whether the data backs that up, using underemployment as the main lens: graduates working jobs that don’t typically need a degree.

Data

  • BLS JOLTS: job openings, hires and layoffs, monthly, 2015–2025
  • NY Fed College Labor Market: unemployment and underemployment for recent grads aged 22–27, 1990–2025
  • NCES Digest of Education Statistics: bachelor’s degrees conferred, 1970–2021
  • Google Trends: six job-search terms such as “entry level jobs” and “years of experience required”, 2015–2025
  • Georgetown CEW + NY Fed Major Explorer: 74 majors matched after harmonizing names

Method

D3.js bubble chartChart.js line & gap chartsannotation layerssource harmonizationscroll storytelling

We built the story as a single-page site with no frameworks: seven Chart.js charts, an interactive unemployment vs. underemployment toggle, and a D3 bubble chart where each major’s size is its early-career wage. Google Trends is used only as corroborating evidence, and we left sparse pre-2005 NCES years uninterpolated.

Results

46%fewer actual hires than job postings impliedBLS JOLTS
~42%of recent graduates are underemployedNY Fed
1.07 → 1.75openings per hire, 2015 to the 2022 peakBLS JOLTS
12.3Mjob openings in March 2022, the highest on recordBLS JOLTS

Bachelor’s completions more than doubled from 839K in 1970 to 2.07M in 2021. Underemployment for liberal arts and general business majors runs two to three times higher than for engineering and nursing.

What it means

Postings overstated real demand. Openings surged after 2021 while hiring did not keep pace, so new graduates competed for fewer real jobs than the headlines suggested.
